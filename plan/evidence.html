Evidence and Decisions

What this plan decides

Conditional go for one paid test: a managed revenue desk for residential heating and cooling service firms. Rocket Station would own and operate it. Pest control is the second choice. Plumbing is third because urgent dispatch raises the cost of mistakes.

This is a proposed offer, not a launched service. No customer interviews, paid commitments, live system tests, or internal cost records were available for this research. Our stated country footprint and team capabilities supplied the starting point. Spare capacity is unverified.

What the two Rocket Station passes establish

Research accessed 22 October 2026. Two separate retrieval passes covered 148 discovered first-party routes. Each returned 147 successful responses and one error route. Redirects reduce the successful corpus to 132 canonical content pages. Both passes archived raw responses, visible text, source-element inventories, retrieval times, and SHA-256 hashes.

Each pass counted 46,448 HTML start elements, including 4,701 paragraphs, 2,585 headings, 7,440 links, 1,330 images, and six tables. These counts include shared navigation and redirect copies. All page element-count inventories match. All 147 successful visible-text hashes match. Raw response hashes differ; randomized email-protection attributes account for observed differences but were not the subject of an exhaustive causal analysis.

Two readers completed two semantic passes of the extracted meaningful text. Core and regional pages were read with shared text deduplicated. Both article groups were read in full twice. Reading maps tie notes to pages, line units, and hashes. The archive proves retrieval and correspondence. Notes record interpretation. Neither can prove perfect comprehension.

The coverage excludes exhaustive inspection of rendered interaction states, script behavior, image pixels, video, canvas, private portals, and external booking or social services. Unlinked pages outside the discovered corpus may exist. The literal claim that every element of the entire rendered website was read twice is not supported.

Facts that change the recommendation

Rocket Station already advertises home-services operations. The current offering includes scheduling, dispatch coordination, customer communication, job records, estimates, and billing. Rocket Nation must test a narrower repeatable offer, rather than claim that entering home services is itself new. Current home-services page, accessed 22 October 2026.

Current commercial pages describe activity-and-volume pricing. They do not publish a usable dollar tariff for the proposed managed service. Older articles publish a $10 hourly rate. That is historical marketing, not a current Orbit quotation or an internal labor-cost benchmark. Current service process, legacy property-management article, accessed 22 October 2026.

The public FAQ contains conflicting responsibility models. Its managed-service section assigns performance oversight to Rocket Station. Its legacy assistant section tells clients to manage the assistant. The signed pilot agreement must resolve this. The plan assumes provider-owned delivery within explicit client approval rules; it does not claim that this is already the company's binding service contract. Rocket Station FAQ, accessed 22 October 2026.

Regional pages and company performance claims are first-party assertions. They do not prove demand in a trade, available staff, independent certification scope, or the economics of this offer.

Financial model boundaries

All prices, costs, workload, churn, acquisition, and conversion rates are assumptions. The interactive model uses the same inputs as the plan. Gross margin includes frontline labor, technology, quality/shift oversight, and direct client support. Contribution also subtracts incremental retention expense. Fixed overhead excludes acquisition payroll and marketing already allocated to customer acquisition cost.

Operating break-even assumes a stable client base and no growth spending. Cash break-even retains the monthly commercial-cost floor and replacement acquisition. The plan also supplies a 24-month stress case with whole-person staffing floors and setup cash. It needs about $838,000 through that checkpoint and is still loss-making there. The $150,000–$250,000 testing and pilot budget is part of launch capital, but covers only the early stages. Taxes, financing, bad debt, and delayed collections would increase cash need.

The market anchor combines plumbing and heating/cooling employer locations. Filters are assumptions. The resulting serviceable pool is a planning proxy, not a measured count of heating-and-cooling buyers.
