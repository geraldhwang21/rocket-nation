Go to Market

Positioning: “A managed revenue desk that completes the calls, follow-up and job paperwork inside your existing system, with a named team and a weekly record of results.” Focus on firms that receive a steady flow of customer enquiries, have documented gaps in follow-up, and have enough technicians available to take on additional jobs. Use their records to test the need; firm size alone does not establish it.

Show valid appointments, completed and collected jobs, contribution from recovered estimates, invoice preparation time, missing records, and client management hours. Use actual results. Compare the offer with software alone, a general assistant, another managed provider, and internal hiring. Test whether trained staff, backup, case review, and completion across several workflows justify the price. Local knowledge and in-person work remain client strengths.

Test social-led inbound first

Prioritize a bounded test of inbound sales leads from social media. Start with owner-focused Instagram Reels for heating and air conditioning businesses in California, New York and Texas. Test whether this reaches paying clients at lower cost and with faster payback than targeted human outreach. Both routes need people to qualify and close.

Inbound: the owner comes to us

Reel → enquiry form → Rocket Station intake in the Philippines → assigned salesperson → paid workflow diagnostic → paid pilot. Intake records the business, contact’s role and operational need; the salesperson qualifies and closes. Target business owners; route homeowner repair enquiries away from this sales process.

Outbound: we approach the owner

A salesperson approaches owners selected by written criteria from the three-state prospect list, using the same offer and sales process. The list supplies research candidates; qualification happens in conversation. Keep a small comparison group alongside the social test.

Choose on paying customers, not views

Track source, qualified conversations, attended diagnostics, paid pilots, renewals at standard price and days to a paid pilot. Compare total cost per paid pilot and per renewed client, including content, advertising, intake, tools, follow-up, selling and management time, and handling unsuitable enquiries. Report organic and paid social separately. Judge each route against actual delivery contribution and the existing pilot scorecard; keep the calculator’s assumptions until evidence supports changing them.

Test setup and intake handoff

Before launch, assign and train intake staff; confirm coverage in the owner’s local time, response targets, missed-call callbacks and an accepted handoff to the salesperson. Use the enquiry form first; add a phone number when coverage is ready. Set the budget, qualification criteria, observation window, payback limit and stop rule before spending. Record state and size where known, use comparable selection criteria, and disclose differences between the groups. Record first contact, mixed-source leads and referrals separately. Small samples give direction; wait for renewal results before scaling. Broad paid advertising stays deferred until the message is repeatable.

Use trusted introductions, trade consultants/implementers and referral partners alongside these tests, with the relationship holder’s authorization and separate source tracking. Request internal permission and check existing agreements before approaching existing Rocket Station customers. Build one reusable workflow diagnostic, one approved call example, one actual case study and one scope/return worksheet. Defer mass lead buying and a national content program until evidence supports a repeatable message.

Before selling the pilot, record five comparisons: current office cost and failure; relevant native software features and whether they are enabled; human exception work left after correct setup; the least costly adequate alternative and why the buyer rejects it; and the overlap with current Orbit scope, price, and delivery cost. Record the specific paid reason to choose this offer. If the gap is only software setup, sell a bounded setup service instead of a recurring desk.

Activation: paid diagnostic → client-approved workflow map → signed narrow scope → access and simulation → limited production traffic → first valid completed handoff. Promise a documented setup sequence, not a universal instant launch. Retention: weekly exception/outcome reviews, monthly scope/usage health check and a named remediation lead. Referral: request introduction only after verified results and client consent. Revenue: volume tier expansion or a proven adjacent module; do not sell unrelated services to rescue weak core economics.

Earlier acquisition example, retained for comparison: $2,000 per month for tools and targeted tests plus an $8,000 seller allocation requires at least 2.22 gross wins per month to achieve $4,500 acquisition cost per win; 45 wins over 24 modeled months produces about $5,333 per win. This is not the current calculator’s Base case. Its Base acquisition cost is $5,500 per new client, including commission and campaign cost plus allocated sales-team and marketing cost. Use the current calculator’s complete inputs when assessing payback; do not combine costs from these two cases. No paid-media or staffing commitment is made. Review completed customer cohorts and actual ledger cost. A new spending decision requires demonstrated conversion and delivery contribution.

Pilot scorecard: three paid clients, controlled observation

Entry criteria: fits trade/size/platform/time zone; prior four weeks of usable records or disclosed missing baseline; enough approved appointment capacity; data/recording permissions and contact rules; named on-call manager; signed scope; real payment.

Run an initial baseline and at least four complete operating weeks under comparable service windows, extending observation if seasonality or too few eligible cases prevents interpretation. Where possible route comparable eligible work between current process and the service; otherwise use matched prior periods and clearly label causal uncertainty. Track call source, job type, field capacity, weather/season effects and pricing changes. A difference from baseline is not automatically caused by Rocket Nation.

The value target means at least $3,742.50/month in documented client benefit at base price; this is a commercial test threshold, not a forecast. If incremental collected contribution per extra completed job is assumed $250, the fee alone requires about ten incremental jobs; the 1.5× value threshold needs about fifteen. Replace $250 with the client's actual collected revenue minus incremental labor/material/other variable cost. Never use ticket value as profit, and never add invoice cash acceleration to revenue as if it were new profit.

Expand only when quality/reliability checks pass, two renewals and economic value are documented, and actual staffing capacity is committed. Revise if price, scope or handling time narrowly misses with a credible corrective test. Stop a workflow immediately for unresolved critical safety/privacy issues; stop the business expansion if two bounded iterations cannot achieve credible value and positive contribution. Pilot-specific subsidy must be reported separately from mature unit economics.

Measurement funnel: researched account → completed qualified conversation → diagnostic → signed paid pilot → activated client → renewal → referral/expansion. Every rate uses matching cohorts and closed observation windows; a buyer still evaluating or pilot still running is pending. Sales materials must contain offer, boundaries, implementation responsibilities, sample scorecard, actual evidence and an objection sheet. Business development validates pain and payment before promising product development.
