Investment Case

Executive Summary

Conditional go: sell a narrow managed revenue desk to residential heating and air conditioning service firms before building a new platform. The provisional customer has 5–20 field technicians, one operating time zone, one existing scheduling system, a named service manager and enough spare field capacity to fulfill recovered demand. We should initially own intake, rule-based booking, estimate follow-up and job-to-invoice completion. The client keeps technical judgment, safety decisions, pricing authority and control of technicians. Confidence is moderate in this direction and low in untested willingness to pay and delivery costs.

Define the package in three parts. First, map the agreed workflows, approval limits and baseline failures during setup. Second, run the bounded desk with trained people, backup coverage and named responsibility for exceptions. Third, report completed handoffs, time to invoice review and client management time against that baseline. These are delivery responsibilities to test in paid pilots, not achieved results. Keep any broader records-cleanup, integration or sale-preparation project separately scoped and priced; do not imply that the base desk includes a company-wide transformation.

Rocket Station already advertises home-services operations through Orbit: scheduling, customer communication, job coordination, billing, and estimates inside existing field-service software. Its website describes contracts priced by activity and volume. That makes a narrower repeatable service worth testing. It does not establish delivery quality, available staff, margins, current certification scope, or rights to reuse client workflows. Rocket Station home-services offer, accessed 22 October 2026.

Fragmentation alone is insufficient. Census reports 111,207 employer establishments in the combined plumbing, heating and air conditioning classification in 2023. That includes commercial and installation businesses and is not a count of qualified buyers. Established software and service providers already address the workflows. The gap to test is accountable execution of the work across calls, follow-up and administrative completion, with measurable results and low client management burden. Census industry profile, 2023 data, accessed 22 October 2026.

Ranked segments: (1) residential heating and air conditioning service/maintenance firms with a managed revenue desk; (2) pest-control firms with recurring-service retention, scheduling and billing administration; (3) plumbing service firms with intake and estimate follow-up while retaining live dispatch with the client. Launch each as a human-led service on the client's software. Launch only the leader initially.

Test a standard offer at $2,495 per location per month plus $1,500 setup: one named 60-hour local service window per week, up to 400 calls and 300 defined administrative tasks monthly. These are proposed prices. The 60 productive human-hour monthly envelope is a workload assumption, not dedicated staffing or unlimited work. After-hours service and broader integration projects require separate scope and cost tests. At the current calculator’s untouched Base settings, pooled delivery cost is about $1,600 per client per month and gross margin is 35.9%. After account-management and integration labor, contribution is about $593 before shared overhead and acquisition spending. Acquisition payback, including the onboarding shortfall, is about 9.72 months. Whole-person steady-state break-even is 87 active clients, including replacement acquisition. This case does not meet the proposed 45% gross-margin launch threshold; validate actual cost and test price or scope before selling it as sustainable.

Name our operating lead, confirm staffed capacity and actual cost, inspect permitted workflow records, and obtain three paid pilots near the target price. The proposed $150,000–$250,000 testing and pilot budget covers early validation, not the operating ramp. The current calculator’s Base settings show about $1.404 million of cumulative net cash required by modeled month 36, with about 52 active clients and a monthly operating loss of about $16,577 at that point. Its separate 240-month search finds a peak cumulative cash need of about $2.175 million at modeled month 127 and no full cash recovery within that search. These are assumption-driven model outputs, not a forecast, schedule or spending approval. Limit the first demand test to the proposed $25,000–$40,000 decision gate; fund later stages only when observed demand, quality and full delivery economics justify them.

Decisions taken as planning defaults: Rocket Station operates the service; Rocket Nation is a proposed customer offer subject to brand clearance; launch one trade, one platform and one time-zone cohort; use human-led service with assistive artificial intelligence; charge subscription plus bounded usage; fund learning in stages. These defaults do not imply a separate legal entity or new software company is needed.

Invalidate the leader if fewer than 3 of 15 qualified buying conversations result in paid pilot commitments near the proposed price; clients cannot document value sufficient to justify that price; steady-state productive handling exceeds 75 hours at the offered workload without viable repricing; or clients require unsafe authority/deep custom integrations to make the service useful. These are proposed decision thresholds. Revise positioning or choose the second segment if demand fails; stop the broad model if quality and economics fail across both narrow tests.
